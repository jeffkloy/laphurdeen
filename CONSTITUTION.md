# THE CONSTITUTION OF THE COMMONWEALTH OF LAPHURDEEN

*Adopted by the Constituent Assembly at Lapentieur — Second Edition (Founding Draft, revised)*

---

## PREAMBLE

We, the people of Laphurdeen —

islanders of the middle sea, come freely from many shores under the Founding Charter to build upon these islands a nation where none stood before, bound by choice rather than by blood;

resolved that no single person shall ever rule us, and that our laws shall be made only by broad and genuine agreement;

determined that every voice may speak freely, that every person may live safely and with dignity regardless of who they are or whom they love, that learning and healing shall be within reach of all, that honest labour shall be honoured and honest government demanded, and that none among us shall be left behind;

do hereby establish this Constitution as the supreme law of the Commonwealth of Laphurdeen.

---

## PART I — THE COMMONWEALTH

### Article 1 — The State
1. Laphurdeen is a sovereign, democratic island commonwealth.
2. The Commonwealth is a **parliamentary consensus democracy**. There shall be no President, Prime Minister, monarch, or any other singular head of state or head of government. Executive authority is exercised collectively as provided in Part V.
3. All sovereignty resides in the people of Laphurdeen, who exercise it through free elections, referendums, and the institutions established by this Constitution.

### Article 2 — Territory and Cities
1. The territory of the Commonwealth comprises the islands of the Laphurdeen archipelago in the central Mediterranean Sea, together with their internal waters, territorial sea, seabed, and airspace.
2. The capital of the Commonwealth is **Lapentieur**.
3. **Darcambria** is recognised as a chartered principal city of the Commonwealth.

### Article 3 — Supremacy of the Constitution
1. This Constitution is the supreme law of Laphurdeen. Any law, act, or omission inconsistent with it is void to the extent of the inconsistency.
2. All public power is held on trust for the people and may be exercised only under this Constitution and law.

### Article 4 — Languages
1. **Laphurdi** is the national language of the Commonwealth. Laphurdi and English are its official languages.
2. Parliament shall maintain a Laphurdi Language Commission to cultivate the national language, and shall protect the linguistic heritage of all communities of Laphurdeen.

## PART II — THE CHARTER OF RIGHTS AND FREEDOMS

### Article 5 — Dignity and Equality
1. The dignity of every person is inviolable.
2. Every person is equal before the law and entitled to its equal protection and benefit without discrimination.
3. Discrimination is prohibited on the grounds of race, ethnicity, national or social origin, colour, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, sex characteristics, disability, age, religion or belief, language, political opinion, property, birth, or socioeconomic status.
4. Marriage and civil union are open to any two consenting adults without distinction of sex, sexual orientation, or gender identity, and confer identical rights and obligations.
5. Every person has the right to legal recognition of their gender identity through a simple administrative act of self-determination.
6. Persons with disabilities have the right to full and effective participation in society on an equal basis with others, including enforceable rights of access to the built environment, transport, information, communications, and services. Reasonable accommodation is a legal duty; its denial is discrimination.
7. The Commonwealth shall take positive measures to achieve substantive equality for women, LGBTQIA+ persons, and persons with disabilities, including in public office, employment, healthcare, and education.

### Article 6 — Freedom of Expression
1. Every person has the right to freedom of thought, opinion, and expression, including the freedom to seek, receive, hold, and impart information and ideas of all kinds, in any form and through any medium, without interference by public authority and regardless of frontiers.
2. No person shall be subject to criminal penalty for the content of expression, except for: direct incitement to imminent violence; true threats; fraud; perjury; the sexual exploitation of children; or the unlawful disclosure of information whose secrecy is strictly necessary for national defence and defined narrowly by law.
3. Defamation is exclusively a civil matter. Criminal defamation, blasphemy, and lèse-majesté offences shall not exist in Laphurdeen.
4. Prior restraint of publication is prohibited save by order of the High Court in the narrowest circumstances prescribed by law.
5. The freedom and pluralism of the press are guaranteed. Journalists have the right to protect their sources.
6. Every person has an enforceable right of access to information held by public bodies, subject only to narrow exceptions prescribed by law and reviewable by the courts.

### Article 7 — Assembly, Association, and Conscience
1. Every person has the right to peaceful assembly and to freedom of association, including the right to form and join political parties, trade unions, and civil organisations.
2. Every person has the right to freedom of conscience and religion, including the freedom to hold no religion. The Commonwealth has no established religion and is neutral among beliefs.

### Article 8 — Privacy and Data
1. Every person has the right to respect for their private and family life, home, and communications.
2. Every person has the right to the protection of their personal data, to know what data public and private bodies hold about them, and to its correction and erasure as provided by law.
3. Mass indiscriminate surveillance of the population is prohibited. Targeted surveillance requires prior judicial authorisation.

### Article 9 — Bodily Autonomy and Health
1. Every person has the right to bodily autonomy and to make free and informed decisions concerning their own body, health, and reproduction.
2. Every resident of Laphurdeen has the right to **universal healthcare**, comprehensive in scope and free at the point of use, funded by the Commonwealth. No resident shall be denied medically necessary care, including mental healthcare, reproductive healthcare, and gender-affirming care, by reason of cost.
3. The Commonwealth shall maintain healthcare standards that rank among the best in the world, and shall report publicly and annually against international benchmarks.
4. Adults have the right to the personal use, possession, and cultivation for personal use of cannabis and other substances classified by law as low-harm (**soft drugs**). Their production and sale are lawful, regulated, and taxed as provided by law. Drug dependence is treated as a matter of health, not of punishment.

### Article 10 — Education
1. Education is a right. Early childhood, primary, and secondary education are free, universal, and of high standard.
2. **Tertiary education shall be low-cost by constitutional guarantee.** Tuition at public tertiary institutions shall not exceed a ceiling fixed by law, which shall never exceed four per cent (4%) of the national median annual income per academic year, and no student shall be required to borrow commercially to meet it.
3. Academic freedom and the autonomy of institutions of higher learning are guaranteed.

### Article 11 — Work and Labour Rights
1. Every worker has the right to just and favourable conditions of work; to a living wage fixed by law and reviewed annually; to limits on working hours; to safe and healthy workplaces; to paid annual, sick, and parental leave; and to equal pay for work of equal value.
2. Every worker has the right to form and join a trade union, to bargain collectively, and to strike. No worker may be dismissed or disadvantaged for lawful union activity.
3. Workers are entitled to representation in the governance of large enterprises as provided by law.
4. **Sex work between consenting adults is lawful work.** Sex workers enjoy the full protection of this Article — including the right to form and join trade unions, to bargain collectively, and to safe conditions — and shall not be subject to criminal penalty, to registration regimes designed to exclude or stigmatise, or to discrimination in housing, banking, or services.

### Article 12 — The Laphurdeen Guarantee (Social Security)
1. The Commonwealth owes a special and enforceable duty of care to the least-advantaged third of its population, determined by a transparent statutory index of income, wealth, disability, and circumstance.
2. Persons within the Guarantee are entitled at minimum to: an income floor sufficient for a dignified life; secure and adequate housing; energy, water, and digital connectivity; and priority access to education, training, and employment services.
3. Funding of the Guarantee is a first charge on the revenues of the Commonwealth, after the service of public debt.

### Article 13 — Freedom of Movement and Public Transport
1. Every person lawfully within Laphurdeen may move freely throughout the Commonwealth and choose their place of residence.
2. Every resident has the right to **public transport**: a frequent, safe, affordable, and fully accessible public transport network reaching substantially the whole territory and population of the Commonwealth, as guaranteed by law.
3. In the planning, funding, and design of public space and infrastructure, public and active transport take priority over private motor transport. The Commonwealth shall report annually on network coverage, frequency, accessibility, and affordability against the best standards in the world.
4. Fares shall be capped by law at a level affordable to all residents, and persons within the Laphurdeen Guarantee travel free of charge.

### Article 14 — Justice
1. Every person is entitled to a fair, public, and timely hearing before an independent and impartial court, to be presumed innocent, and to legal counsel at public expense where they cannot afford it.
2. Torture, and cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment or punishment, are absolutely prohibited.
3. The death penalty is abolished and may never be reintroduced.
4. Detention is subject to prompt judicial review. Imprisonment shall aim at rehabilitation.

### Article 15 — Limitation of Rights
1. The rights in this Charter may be limited only by law, only so far as is necessary and proportionate in a free and democratic society founded on dignity, equality, and freedom, and only consistently with the essence of each right.
2. The rights in Articles 5(1), 14(2), and 14(3), and the freedom of inner thought and conscience, are absolute and may not be limited or derogated from under any circumstance, including emergency.

## PART III — PARLIAMENT

### Article 16 — Structure
1. The legislative power of the Commonwealth vests in the **Parliament of Laphurdeen**, comprising two chambers: **the Senate** and **the Commons**.
2. Every member of Parliament is elected by the people through direct popular vote.

### Article 17 — The Senate
1. The Senate comprises **one Senator for every one hundred thousand residents** of the Commonwealth, the number of seats being fixed after each census by the Electoral and Qualifications Commission.
2. Senate seats are apportioned among the Ministries by Act of Parliament, each Ministry receiving at least one seat.
3. Every Senator is a **Lead Minister** of the Ministry to which their seat is assigned. The Lead Ministers of a Ministry together form its **Ministerial College**, direct the Ministry collegially, and divide its portfolios of responsibility among themselves as provided by law.
4. Each Senator is elected to a named seat within a Ministry by national popular vote, using ranked-choice ballot, for a term of four years. The people of Laphurdeen thereby directly elect every Lead Minister.
5. A candidate for a Senate seat must satisfy the professional and integrity qualifications for that Ministry fixed by the Electoral and Qualifications Commission, applied identically to all candidates.
6. A Senator who ceases to hold office as a Lead Minister ceases to be a Senator, and the seat is filled by by-election within ninety days.

### Article 18 — The Commons
1. The Commons comprises **three members for each province** of the Commonwealth.
2. Members of the Commons are elected by the electors of their province, using the ranked-choice (single transferable) vote, for terms of four years.
3. Upon taking their seat, each member of the Commons assumes and holds a **managerial office or above within a Ministry**, with duties, reporting lines, and protected legislative time fixed by law. A member who ceases to hold such office ceases to be a member.
4. Candidates for the Commons must satisfy the professional and integrity qualifications for managerial service in a Ministry, fixed by the Electoral and Qualifications Commission.

### Article 19 — The Consensus Rule
1. No bill becomes law unless it is passed **in each chamber by not less than sixty-six per cent (66%) of the votes cast**, a quorum of two-thirds of the membership of that chamber being present.
2. Every vote of every member is free and recorded. Binding instructions to members by any party, employer, or other body are void.

### Article 20 — Resolution of Deadlock
1. If a bill passes one chamber but fails or is not considered in the other within ninety days, a Conciliation Committee of equal members from each chamber shall seek a common text.
2. A conciliated text returns to both chambers under Article 19. If it again fails, Parliament in joint sitting may, by sixty-six per cent of votes cast, refer the bill to a binding national referendum.
3. A bill approved at referendum by a majority of votes cast, on a turnout of at least half of registered electors, becomes law.

### Article 21 — Promulgation
Bills duly passed are certified by the presiding officers of both chambers and promulgated by the Convenor of the Senate within fourteen days. **No person or office holds a power of veto over an act of Parliament.**

### Article 22 — Elections, Recall, and the Electoral Commission
1. Every citizen aged eighteen years or over has the right to vote, by ranked-choice ballot. Voting is accessible to all, including persons with disabilities, and no elector shall face material barriers to the franchise.
2. Elections are administered by an independent **Electoral and Qualifications Commission**, which also fixes and publishes the qualification standards under Articles 17 and 18, subject to review by the High Court.
3. Electors of the Commonwealth may recall any member of Parliament by petition of twenty per cent of registered electors of the relevant electorate, followed by a recall vote requiring a sixty per cent majority.
4. Campaign finance is publicly funded and privately capped as fixed by law; all contributions are publicly disclosed in real time.

## PART IV — THE MINISTRIES

### Article 23 — The Ministries of the Commonwealth
1. There are established twelve Ministries:
   1. Ministry of Justice and Rights
   2. Ministry of Public Safety and Integrity
   3. Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade
   4. Ministry of Finance and the Treasury
   5. Ministry of Health
   6. Ministry of Education and Research
   7. Ministry of Social Welfare and Housing
   8. Ministry of Labour and Economic Development
   9. Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport
   10. Ministry of Environment and Marine Resources
   11. Ministry of Culture, Media and Digital Society
   12. Ministry of Defence and Civil Protection
2. Ministries may be created, merged, renamed, or dissolved only by Act of Parliament under the Consensus Rule, taking effect from the next general election.

### Article 24 — The Civil Service
1. The civil service of Laphurdeen is professional, merit-based, and politically impartial, save for the elected offices established by Articles 17 and 18.
2. Appointment and promotion within the civil service are by open merit. Patronage appointments are prohibited and void.

---

## PART V — EXECUTIVE AUTHORITY

### Article 25 — Collective Executive
1. The executive authority of the Commonwealth vests in **the Senate acting collectively as the Council of the Commonwealth**.
2. Decisions of the Council require the agreement of sixty-six per cent of Senators. Within that collective frame, each Ministerial College directs the day-to-day administration of its Ministry under law and within the appropriated budget.
3. The Council and every Minister are accountable to the Commons, which may summon any Minister, require documents, and conduct inquiries.

### Article 26 — The Convenor of the Senate
1. The Senate has a **Convenor**, elected annually by the Senate from among its members by sixty-six per cent of votes cast. No Senator may serve more than two terms as Convenor within a single Parliament.
2. The Convenor presides over the Senate, promulgates laws, accredits and receives ambassadors, and performs the ceremonial and representative functions of the Commonwealth abroad and at home.
3. The Convenor is first among equals, holds no executive power beyond their own ministerial office, and acts internationally only on the instruction of the Council.

### Article 27 — Foreign Affairs and Defence
1. Treaties are negotiated on the authority of the Council and bind the Commonwealth only upon ratification by both chambers under the Consensus Rule.
2. Laphurdeen maintains defence forces under strict civilian control, directed by the Ministerial College for Defence and Civil Protection within policy set by the Council.
3. No deployment of force beyond the territory of the Commonwealth, and no declaration of a state of war, is lawful without the approval of both chambers under the Consensus Rule.

---

## PART VI — THE JUDICIARY

### Article 28 — Judicial Power
1. The judicial power of the Commonwealth vests in the **High Court of Laphurdeen** and such other courts as Parliament establishes by law.
2. The courts are independent. No person or body may direct a court in the exercise of its judicial function.

### Article 29 — Appointment and Tenure
1. Judges are nominated by an independent **Judicial Appointments Commission** on published merit criteria and confirmed by Parliament in joint sitting by sixty-six per cent of votes cast.
2. Judges hold office until the age of seventy and may be removed only for proven incapacity or serious misconduct, on the report of a judicial tribunal and a sixty-six per cent vote of Parliament in joint sitting.

### Article 30 — Constitutional Review
1. The High Court is the final interpreter of this Constitution and may declare invalid any law or act inconsistent with it.
2. Any person whose Charter rights are affected has standing to seek review.

---

## PART VII — INTEGRITY AND ANTI-CORRUPTION

### Article 31 — The Integrity Commission
1. There is an independent **Integrity Commission of Laphurdeen** with power to investigate corruption, abuse of office, and misuse of public funds by any person exercising public functions, including members of Parliament and judges.
2. The Commission has the powers of a standing royal commission, reports publicly, and refers criminal matters for independent prosecution.
3. Its commissioners are appointed in the manner of judges under Article 29 and are removable only in the same manner.

### Article 32 — Transparency of Public Life
1. Every member of Parliament, judge, commissioner, and senior official shall maintain a **public register of assets, interests, income, and gifts**, updated within thirty days of any material change.
2. All lobbying of public officials is recorded in a public register. Secret lobbying is an offence.
3. Public contracts, budgets, and expenditure are published in open, machine-readable form.

### Article 33 — Whistleblowers, the Auditor-General, and the Public Advocate
1. Any person who in good faith discloses wrongdoing in public or private institutions is protected by law from retaliation of any kind.
2. An independent **Auditor-General** audits all public bodies and reports directly to Parliament and the public.
3. An independent **Public Advocate** (ombudsman) investigates maladministration on complaint by any person, without charge.

---

## PART VIII — PUBLIC FINANCE

### Article 34 — Taxation
1. Tax may be imposed only by Act of Parliament.
2. The annual income of natural persons above a **tax-free threshold of one hundred thousand kronur (Kr. 100,000)** is taxed at a single flat rate of **twenty-five per cent (25%)**; income at or below the threshold is untaxed. The profits of businesses are taxed at a single flat rate of **twelve and one-half per cent (12.5%)**. No other rate, surcharge, or bracket may be applied to income or profits.
3. The rates in clause 2 may be altered only by constitutional amendment under Article 43. Parliament may by law raise the tax-free threshold, but may never lower it below one hundred thousand kronur.
4. Parliament may by law impose duties, consumption taxes, and charges directed at harm or scarcity, provided the overall burden of taxation remains transparent and published annually per household decile.

### Article 35 — The Budget and Continuity of Government
1. The annual budget is proposed by the Ministerial College for Finance on behalf of the Council and passed as a law under the Consensus Rule.
2. **If a budget is not passed before the financial year begins, the previous budget continues in force**, adjusted for inflation by a formula fixed in law, until a new budget is passed. The operations of the Commonwealth shall never cease for want of an appropriation.

### Article 36 — Household Debt
1. It is a constitutional objective of the Commonwealth that its residents carry the **lowest household debt-to-income burden among developed nations**.
2. Parliament shall by law cap the total cost of consumer credit, prohibit predatory lending, guarantee free financial counselling, and require the annual publication of national and decile household debt-to-income statistics.

### Article 37 — Currency and the Reserve Bank
1. The currency of the Commonwealth is the **Laphurdeen krona** (symbol **Kr.**; plural **kronur**).
2. The krona is maintained at a fixed exchange rate of **one hundred kronur (Kr. 100) to one United States dollar (US$1)**. The peg may be altered only by Act of Parliament under the Consensus Rule, upon the published advice of the Reserve Bank.
3. An independent **Reserve Bank of Laphurdeen** has the sole right to issue the currency, maintains the peg and the stability of the financial system, and reports publicly to Parliament at least quarterly. Its Governor is appointed in the manner of judges under Article 29.

---

## PART IX — CITIZENSHIP AND RESIDENCY

### Article 38 — Citizenship
1. Citizenship of Laphurdeen is acquired by birth in the Commonwealth to at least one parent who is a citizen or permanent resident; by descent; or by naturalisation after five years of lawful residence, on conditions fixed by law.
2. Dual and multiple citizenship are permitted. No citizen may be deprived of citizenship so as to be rendered stateless, nor for the expression of opinion.

### Article 39 — Residents
Lawful residents who are not citizens enjoy all rights in this Constitution except the franchise and eligibility for elected office, which Parliament may extend to long-term residents by law.

---

## PART X — PROVINCES AND LOCAL GOVERNMENT

### Article 40 — Provinces and Chartered Cities
1. The territory of the Commonwealth is divided into **provinces**, established and altered by Act of Parliament on the recommendation of an independent Boundary Commission, having regard to community, geography, and reasonable equality of population.
2. Each province elects three members of the Commons under Article 18.
3. **Lapentieur** and **Darcambria** are chartered cities, each with an elected council and mayor and with powers of local self-government fixed by charter law.
4. Government of the Commonwealth is conducted on the principle of subsidiarity: decisions are taken as close to the people affected as good government allows.

---

## PART XI — EMERGENCIES

### Article 41 — States of Emergency
1. The Council may declare a state of emergency only in the case of war, invasion, epidemic, or catastrophe threatening the life of the nation, by a sixty-six per cent vote of the Senate.
2. A declaration lapses after seven days unless ratified by the Commons by sixty-six per cent of votes cast, and thereafter after thirty days unless renewed in the same manner.
3. Emergency measures must be strictly required, proportionate, time-bound, and subject at all times to review by the High Court. The rights identified in Article 15(2) are non-derogable. Parliament may not be suspended and elections may be postponed by no more than ninety days.

---

## PART XII — AMENDMENT

### Article 42 — Ordinary Amendment
This Constitution may be amended by a bill passed in each chamber by **eighty per cent** of the votes cast and approved at a national referendum by a majority of votes cast, on a turnout of at least half of registered electors.

### Article 43 — Entrenched Provisions
An amendment touching the Charter of Rights and Freedoms (Part II), the Consensus Rule (Article 19), the prohibition of a singular executive (Article 1(2)), the tax rates (Article 34(2)), or this Article, requires in addition approval at referendum by **sixty per cent** of votes cast.

---

## PART XIII — TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

### Article 44 — Commencement
1. This Constitution enters into force upon its adoption by the Constituent Assembly and approval at a founding referendum.
2. All persons enrolled under the Founding Charter, and their children, are citizens of Laphurdeen from commencement.
3. Before the first general election, the Constituent Assembly shall by law establish the first provinces and the first apportionment of Senate seats among the Ministries.
4. The first general election shall be held within one hundred and eighty days of commencement, administered by an interim Electoral and Qualifications Commission appointed by the Constituent Assembly.
5. Until the first Parliament assembles, the Constituent Assembly exercises legislative power under the Consensus Rule, and an interim Council of portfolio administrators exercises executive power collectively.

*Done at Lapentieur, for the people of Laphurdeen.*
