# Darcambria — the Province of the Amber Curve

*dar* + *cambria*: "the amber curve" (see `LAPHURDI.md` §7). The great
curved bay is the founding Anchorage — the water on the flag — and the
province is everything the bay gathers: eight Charter cities around the
shore, the Peaklands' gorges rising behind, and **4,900,000** people, the
better half of them on one island.

## The Province and the Communities

The eight Charter cities govern together as **the Darcambrian
Communities** — the "DC" of the DCTS (see `TRANSPORT.md`) — with the
Government Centre on Darlingmoors island. Their names came ashore with the
English fleet and, like the ⟨ph⟩ in Laphurdeen, were never touched by the
Reform.

By population: Darlingmoors, Addison, Marionberry, Upperlea, Lowerlea,
Briarside, Winchester-on-the-Sea, Riverlin.

By density: Darlingmoors, Riverlin, Addison, Marionberry, Lowerlea,
Briarside, Winchester-on-the-Sea, Upperlea — Riverlin the smallest by
population and second by density, a city that packs its riverbank tight.

## Darlingmoors — City & Island

Population **2,850,000**: the largest city in the Commonwealth, and its
chartered principal city under Article 2 of the Constitution. Darlingmoors
is an island in the bay, and the city is the island — five zones and a
City Centre, each with its own dialling code under the 0221 plan:

| Zone | Code | Districts |
|---|---|---|
| City Centre | 0221 2 | City Centre |
| C | 0221 3 | Outer Docks, Government Centre, Inner Docks, Yacht Harbour |
| NW | 0221 4 | Seaport Village, The Northwest, Outlook, Darling Hill, Timberland |
| NE | 0221 5 | Oyster Point, Forest Park, The Beaches |
| SW | 0221 6 | Peak District, Eastern Peak, Inner Gorge, Outer Gorge |
| SE | 0221 7 | Southeast Slopes, Shoreline Rocks, Cambrian Junction |

The City Centre Mainline — stations Central, Market Square, Old
Courthouse — is where Baaner Laphurdeen's express triangle meets the T.

## The Financial Centre

Market Square is the money quarter of the Commonwealth. On its east side
stands **the Bors** — the Darlingmoors Exchange, the trading floor under
the amber-glass dome, where the province's grain, salt, and shipping have
been priced since the Charter era. Along the north side run the bank
headquarters, English brick over French stone. And in the square's oldest
corner works **the Mint**, striking the three words of the motto —
*Frihed, Velvard, Konsens* — onto every krona the Reserve Bank of
Laphurdeen issues, at the pegged Kr. 100 to the dollar that Article 38
guarantees and every visitor quietly appreciates.

The quarter keeps market hours, not banking hours: the Square's morning
bell still opens trading, and the bell is older than the country.

## Arts & Culture

Old Courthouse gave up the law to the arts a century ago. The courthouse
itself is now **the Galleri av de Mange Strander** — the national
collection, named like the anthem for the many shores — and the streets
around it hold the small museums: the Charter Fleet rooms, the print
workshops, the Reform-era Language Commission's first offices kept as
they were left.

At Yacht Harbour the water does the staging: **the Harbour Opera** sits
on the mole with the bay for a backdrop, and **the Amber Hall** — the
Communities' konsert hall — floats its Sunday programme out over the
marina on summer evenings. Between the two, Government Centre's plaza
carries the free festivals: the Charter Days in spring, the anthem sung
by eight cities' choirs at once.

## The Music Scene

The other music lives in the SE. **Cambrian Junction** is warehouse
country — the sheds along the old freight wye are the venues now, loud
after dark, painted by whoever played last; the Junction's New Year
all-nighter is a rite of passage the rest of the province pretends to
disapprove of. **Seaport Village**, at the opposite corner of the island,
answers quietly: folk rooms over the chandleries, settler ballads in
three languages, the fiddle tunes the ferry families brought south.

Between them the city hums — buskers on the Mainline concourses are
licensed, tolerated, and occasionally famous.

## The Food Scene

Darlingmoors eats in the street and dines on the water:

- **Oyster Point** is the *ostra* coast — the shacks on the point serve
  them by the dozen with Saltvik salt and Vinedale white, and the
  morning boats sell straight off the transom.
- **The market halls** behind Market Square run stalls the width of the
  Mediterranean: grilled *fisk*, flatbreads, the Communities' eight
  rival versions of the same fish soup.
- **The Beaches boardwalk** fries what the R6 brings in — paper cones,
  amber sunsets, no cutlery.
- **Yacht Harbour** is the *restoran* row, high register in both
  languages: white cloth, bay views, and the tax already in the price,
  as Article 14's cousin in the price laws demands of every menu in the
  country.

Mediterranean produce through English, French, and Norse settler
kitchens: that is the Darlingmoors table.

## The Beaches

The NE zone earns its district name. **The Beaches** run three
kilometres of city sand on the U2 and R6 lines — the only capital-class
beach in Europe you can reach by tram-token prices, as the tourist board
never tires of saying. **Shoreline Rocks**, in the SE, is the swimmers'
coast: deep coves, diving platforms, the morning club that swims the
year round. And the **Forest Park coast** is the quiet third — pine
shade to the tide line, no boardwalk, no fry stands, the city's best
kept unsecret.

## The Suburban Zones

Beyond the island, the Communities' suburbs run in two arcs: SW from
Winchester-on-the-Sea to Riverlin, SE from Linear Village to Briarside
South — the T's U1 and the x-expresses stitch them to the Mainline.
