Fra mange strander

The Anchorage

Laphurdeen means “The Anchorage.” Old Charter La Fjärde - a French article wrapped around a Swedish word for a wide, sheltered bay - became Laphurde, and the archaic definite ending -een made it a name. It commemorates the bay where the founding fleet first dropped anchor.

Four fleets came - English, Dutch, Swedish, and French-speaking - and chose to be one people. The Constitution’s preamble still says it plainly: a nation built “bound by choice rather than by blood.” You can hear all four shores in every sentence of Laphurdi, the national language they braided together.

Lapentieur “the place of slopes”

The capital. A fog-brushed hill city stacked above its harbour - French la pente, “the slope,” plus the Old Charter place-suffix -ieur. The white star on the flag is Lapentieur’s harbour light, the star the Charter fleet steered by.

Darlingmoors city & island in the amber curve

The chartered principal city, and the Commonwealth’s largest: the city is an island in Darcambria’s great curved bay. At dusk the water turns amber - that is the arc on the flag, and the bay that gave it its colour.

Agaetisboro “Alright-town”

The resort city on the island's southern tip: three hundred days of sun, white-sand beaches, two casinos, and the Commonwealth's biggest fishing fleet. The first fishers to round the cape sent back a single word - agaet, “alright.” It never needed more.

Konsensdemokrati

A nation with no ruler

Article 1 of the Constitution is blunt: “There shall be no President, Prime Minister, monarch, or any other singular head of state or head of government.” Laphurdeen is a parliamentary consensus democracy - power is held collectively, exercised openly, and lent, never owned.

0 singular rulers - forbidden by Article 1
66% of votes in both chambers to pass any law - Article 20
12 ministries, each run by a college of elected ministers - Article 24
100% of Parliament directly elected by ranked-choice vote - Article 17

You elect the ministers themselves

Every Senator is a Lead Minister, elected by national popular vote to a named seat in a specific ministry (Article 18). Nobody appoints the health minister here - the country votes them in, by name.

The Commons does real work

Three members per province, and every one of them holds a working managerial office inside a ministry while serving (Article 19). No career spectators.

A head of state who only waves

The Speaker of the Commons is the ceremonial head of state - presiding, promulgating, receiving ambassadors - and holds no executive power at all (Article 27).

No veto - save the people’s.

Once both chambers agree by 66%, a bill becomes law. “No person or office holds a power of veto over an act of Parliament” (Article 22) - none, that is, but the people’s own: two per cent of electors can send a new law to referendum, and five per cent can propose an amendment to the Constitution itself (Part XIV). If the chambers deadlock, the people settle it by referendum (Article 21).

Glass-walled government

Every legislator’s assets sit in a public register, all lobbying is recorded, campaign money is disclosed in real time, and an Integrity Commission with the powers of a standing royal commission watches everyone - including judges (Articles 32–33).

And you can take it all back

Electors can recall any member of Parliament, mid-term, by petition and recall vote (Article 23). Consent here is continuous, not quadrennial.

Frihed, Velvard, Konsens

Three words on every coin

The national motto isn’t decoration - it’s the Constitution’s table of contents.

Frihed freedom

  • No criminal penalty for the content of speech - no blasphemy law, no lèse-majesté, ever (Art. 7).
  • A free and plural press, with protected sources (Art. 7).
  • Mass surveillance is constitutionally prohibited (Art. 9).
  • Believe anything, or nothing: the Commonwealth has no established religion (Art. 8).

Velvard welfare

  • Universal healthcare, comprehensive and free at the point of use (Art. 10).
  • University tuition capped at 4% of median income per year (Art. 11).
  • The Laphurdeen Guarantee: an enforceable duty of care to the least-advantaged quarter and to every person with a disability (Art. 13).
  • A living wage, reviewed annually, for every worker (Art. 12).

Konsens consensus

  • No law passes without 66% of votes cast in each chamber (Art. 20).
  • Every vote of every member is free and recorded (Art. 20).
  • Deadlocks go to the people by binding referendum (Art. 21).
  • Amending the Constitution takes 80% of Parliament and a national referendum (Art. 43).

From the Charter of Rights and Freedoms

  • “The dignity of every person is inviolable.” - Art. 6(1)
  • Marriage and civil union open to any two consenting adults - Art. 6(4)
  • Gender recognition by simple self-determination - Art. 6(5)
  • Accessibility is an enforceable right; denying accommodation is discrimination - Art. 6(6)
  • The death penalty is abolished and may never be reintroduced - Art. 15(3)
  • Prison exists to bring people back to good lives - Art. 15(4)
  • The police carry no firearms, and every bullet the state fires is publicly accounted for - Art. 15(5)-(6)
  • Dual citizenship welcome; no one may be made stateless - Art. 39
  • Taxes are assessed in real time; no one has to file a return - Art. 35(8)

Practicalities, constitutionally guaranteed

Rights you can feel on holiday

The price is the price

Sales tax is included in every displayed price by constitutional command (Art. 35(4)). The number on the menu is the number on the bill. Tipping is a kindness, not a rescue.

You won’t need a car

Frequent, fully accessible public transport reaching substantially the whole Commonwealth is a constitutional right, with fares capped by law (Art. 14). Ferries count.

Arithmetic-free money

The krona is pegged at Kr. 100 = € 1 (Art. 38). A Kr. 450 harbour lunch is €4.50 - divide by one hundred and order another bier.

Two languages, one welcome

Laphurdi is the national language; English is co-official (Art. 4). Every sign, timetable, and menu speaks both. Try the Laphurdi anyway - it opens doors.

Constitutional guarantees for every day

Rights you live with

The officer carries no firearm

Police do not carry firearms - only a special operations unit named by law may (Art. 15(5)). And if the state ever shoots, every bullet is accounted for in a public report: why that bullet, into that organ (Art. 15(6)).

The tax return that doesn't exist

Taxes are assessed in real time, as income arrives and sales are made (Art. 35(8)). Nobody has to file a return - though you may, for a correction or a refund. Wealth above Kr. 100,000,000 pays 0.75% a year (Art. 35(5)).

The debt you never drown in

It is a constitutional objective that residents carry the lowest household debt burden among developed nations (Art. 37): credit costs are capped, predatory lending is banned, and financial counselling is free.

The rights that survive anything

Dignity, the torture ban, the death penalty's abolition, and inner conscience are absolute - not even a state of emergency touches them (Art. 16(2)). And Parliament can never be closed (Art. 42).
