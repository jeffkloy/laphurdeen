Laphurdeen · Values

Frihed, Velvard, Konsens · Freedom, Welfare, Consensus

The values of the Commonwealth

Three words on every coin, a Preamble that reads like a promise, and a Charter that keeps it. This page walks the founding texts - in both of the languages that wrote them.

The motto

Three words, always in Laphurdi

Frihed, Velvard, Konsens - freedom, welfare, consensus. The motto appears on the coat of arms, on every krona coin and note, and on the passport cover, and the First Symbols Act is strict about one thing: it is always rendered in Laphurdi. The English translation may stand beside it in official documents - it never replaces it.

The three words are not decoration; they are the Constitution's table of contents. Freedom is the Charter of Rights and Freedoms. Welfare is the Laphurdeen Guarantee and everything that funds it. Consensus is a Parliament where no law passes without sixty-six per cent of both chambers - and where nobody, ever, holds a veto.

The Preamble

The promise before the articles

Article 4 makes Laphurdi and English equal official languages, and the Constitution exists whole in both - Grundlojen and the Constitution, equally valid. This is the first place the two texts stand together.

Preambelen Laphurdi

Vi, folket av Laphurdeen -

ilander av den Middel Zee, kom fri fra mange strander under Grundsjartan te bygga on dise ilander en nasjon waar ingen stod befor, bindat av veljing og nit av blod;

fast in vaar tro dat ingen enkel person skal aldri regera over os, og dat vaar lojer skal bliva skrivat bara med bred og sant konsens;

fast in vaar vilja dat alle stemmer kan spreka fri; dat alle personer kan leva seker og med verdighed, lik wie dei er og wie dei lievar; dat lering og helsa skal staa open for alle; dat erlig werk skal bliva erat og erlig styring skal bliva kravat; og dat ingen av os skal bliva lemnat efter;

grundar vi hier dis Grundloj als den hoogest loj av Samveldet Laphurdeen.

The Preamble English

We, the people of Laphurdeen -

islanders of the middle sea, come freely from many shores under the Founding Charter to build upon these islands a nation where none stood before, bound by choice rather than by blood;

resolved that no single person shall ever rule us, and that our laws shall be made only by broad and genuine agreement;

determined that every voice may speak freely, that every person may live safely and with dignity regardless of who they are or whom they love, that learning and healing shall be within reach of all, that honest labour shall be honoured and honest government demanded, and that none among us shall be left behind;

do hereby establish this Constitution as the supreme law of the Commonwealth of Laphurdeen.

Part II of the Constitution

The Charter, article by article

Eleven articles, Articles 6 to 16. Each card opens with the Grundlojen's own words.

Art. 6 · Dignity and equality

«Verdigheden av alle personer er absolut.»

Everyone is equal before the law, and discrimination is banned on every ground the Charter can name. Marriage and civil union are open to any two consenting adults; gender recognition is a simple act of self-determination; accessibility is an enforceable right.

Art. 7 · Expression

«Ingen person skal bliva straffat for det dei sejar el skrivar.»

No criminal penalty for the content of speech - no blasphemy law, no lèse-majesté, ever. The press is free and plural, sources are protected, and everyone may see the information public bodies hold.

Art. 8 · Assembly and conscience

«Samveldet har ingen relisjon self, og staar lik mellan alle troer.»

Peaceful assembly, parties, trade unions, and civil associations are rights. Believe anything - or nothing: the Commonwealth has no established religion and stands neutral among beliefs.

Art. 9 · Privacy and data

«Overvaking av alle folket, bred og uten maal, er forbidat.»

Private life, home, and communications are respected. You may know what data anyone holds about you, and have it corrected or erased. Mass surveillance is prohibited; targeted surveillance needs a judge's yes first.

Art. 10 · Body and health

«Adiksjon er en sak for helsa, nit for straff.»

Bodily autonomy is yours. Healthcare is universal, comprehensive, and free at the point of use - mental, reproductive, and gender-affirming care included. Soft drugs are lawful and regulated; dependence is treated as health, never punishment.

Art. 11 · Learning

«Lering er en rekt.»

School is free from the earliest years to the last. University tuition may never exceed four per cent of the median annual income per year, and no student can be forced to borrow commercially. Academic freedom is constitutional.

Art. 12 · Work

«Seksuell werk mellan vuksener med konsens er lojlig werk.»

A living wage fixed by law and reviewed every year; limits on hours; paid leave of every kind; equal pay for equal work. Unions, collective bargaining, and the strike are protected - and sex work between consenting adults is lawful work with all of it.

Art. 13 · The Guarantee

«En inkomst for et verdig liv; et seker og goed hem.»

The Laphurdeen Guarantee: an enforceable duty of care to the least-advantaged quarter of the population and to every person with a disability. Its funding stands first in the budget, after only the public debt.

Art. 14 · Movement and transport

«Personer in Garantien resar gratis.»

Move freely, live where you choose - and public transport is a right: frequent, safe, affordable, fully accessible, reaching substantially the whole country, with fares capped by law.

Art. 15 · Justice

«Ingen vapen blivar byggat in Laphurdeen.»

Fair, public, timely trials, with counsel paid for those who cannot pay; torture absolutely prohibited; the death penalty abolished forever; prison aimed at rehabilitation. And Laphurdeen is disarmed: the police carry no firearms, no person may own one - not even for self-defence - no weapon is manufactured here, and every bullet the state ever fires is publicly accounted for.

Art. 16 · The limits of limits

«Aldri so mykke dat hjertat av rekten stervar.»

Rights may be limited only by law, only as far as necessary and proportionate - never so far that the heart of the right dies. And dignity, the torture ban, the death penalty's abolition, and inner conscience are absolute, even in an emergency.

Beyond Part II

Values the rest of the Constitution keeps

From the other eleven parts

  • No law without 66% of both chambers; deadlocks go to the people by binding referendum - Art. 20-21
  • No person and no office holds a veto over an Act of Parliament - Art. 22
  • Glass-walled public life: open registers, published lobbying, machine-readable budgets, independent watchdogs - Art. 32-34
  • The quiet tax office: rates fixed in the Constitution, prices always tax-included, assessment in real time with no return to file, and wealth above Kr. 100,000,000 paying 0.75% a year - Art. 35
  • The state never stops for want of a budget - Art. 36
  • Emergencies on a leash: necessary, proportionate, end-dated - and Parliament can never be closed - Art. 42

Sang av de Mange Strander

Many shores, one anchorage

The anthem has four verses and a chorus. Each verse arrives from one of the four founding shores - English, Dutch, Swedish, French - a sea-shanty line telling one crossing in its own language. Then the whole Commonwealth answers together, as a hymn, in Laphurdi.

«Fra mange strander kom vi fri,
over vind og vaag og natt;
hoe fjern vi kom, hoe bred den zee -
vaar anker liggar fast.
Mange strander, en hamn:
vi kallar endaa Laphurdeen hem.»

“From many shores we came free, over wind and wave and night; however far we came, however wide the sea - our anchor lies fast. Many shores, one anchorage: we still call Laphurdeen home.” - the chorus, fixed by the First Symbols Act

The chorus quotes the Preamble - «kom fri fra mange strander» - and its last line performs the founding idea one more time: Laphurdeen means The Anchorage, so the anthem names the country and calls it home in the same breath.

The full texts are public, in both official languages - read them side by side, or learn the language they answer in.
