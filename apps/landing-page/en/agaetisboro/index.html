Agaetisboro

The Sun Side of the Commonwealth. Two hundred thousand people at the island's southern tip, three hundred days of sun — and when the first fishers rounded the cape and saw the harbour, they sent one word back to the fleet: «Agaet». Alright.

From the south coast

The Beaches

When the first fishers sailed south around the cape, the fleet asked what they had found. The answer came back as a single old word: «Agaet» — “alright.” Sun the whole year, white sand, a sheltered harbour — and the Commonwealth's greatest understatement became the city's name: Alright-town.

Today Agaetisboro is the Commonwealth's holiday paradise: hotels on the waterfront, coffee-houses on the sand, music in the evening — and always the sea, warm and clear, from morning to night.

Solstranden Sun Beach

Five kilometres of white sand from the harbour to the South Cape. Swim in the warm water, wander the shore road between the coffee-houses — or simply lie in the sun until the evening comes.

Korallviken Coral Cove

Beneath the waves lives the coral reef. Dive among corals of every colour, swim with the fish — the water is so clear you can see it all from the boat.

Sydkapen the South Cape

The cliffs stand high over the sea south of the city. Walk out on the cape in the evening and watch the sun go down amber and round into the water — no evening is more beautiful.

Games, light, and luck

The two casinos

When the sun has gone into the water, Agaetisboro opens its other side: two casinos — one for the sun, one for the moon.

Solhjulet — The Sun Wheel

On Sun Beach stands Solhjulet, with music, food straight from the sea, and the great wheel that gave the house its name. This is where the city plays, from evening to morning.

Maanhuset — The Moon House

Out on the South Cape stands Maanhuset: the formal one, open from the last of the evening light until morning. From your table you watch the moon over the open sea.

And when the house wins?

The game has a ceiling set by law — and the casinos' taxes flow to the welfare system and the Laphurdeen Guarantee. When the house wins, the people win too.

Before the sun, before everyone

The fishing fleet

The hotels are new; the fishery is as old as the city itself. Agaetisboro's fishing fleet — the largest in the Commonwealth — puts out before the sun and comes back with the day's catch for the market by the harbour.

Want to come along? The fishers take travellers out on the run: out with the boat before morning, back with the catch before breakfast. In the evening you eat your own catch on the beach — from the sea to the table in a single day.

Article 14, in the sun too

The Strandbaan — and the way south

One line is all the city needs: the Strandbaan runs from the fish market by the harbour, out along all the beaches, to the Moon House on the South Cape — every ten minutes, morning to night.

You reach Agaetisboro over the sea or through the air: ferries run from Darcambria and Lapentieur every day, and the airport is ten minutes from town on the Strandbaan. Fares are capped by law, and anyone in the Laphurdeen Guarantee rides free (Article 14).
