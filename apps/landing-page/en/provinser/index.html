The Provinces of Laphurdeen

Sixteen provinces from the amber curve to the outer isles — each with three voices in the Commons (Art. 41). This is the map of Laphurdeen: cities, harbours, and the many shores.

The bay and the mountains

The Heartland

Darcambria Darlingmoors

The amber curve: eight Charter cities around the founding bay, and Darlingmoors — the largest city in the Commonwealth. 4,900,000 people.

National Capital Region Lapentieur

The capital on its fog-brushed slopes: the ministries, the Founders' Hills, and morning light on the hills. 1,250,000 people.

Peaklands Highbridge

Peaks and deep gorges behind the amber curve; Highbridge stands on its old Charter-era stone bridge.

Sun and salt

The South

Southcape Agaetisboro

Sun the whole year, white sand and coral — and the casinos of Agaetisboro, the city of the sun.

Saltmarsken Saltvik

The salt marsh seasons the whole country's table; the flamingos come for the winter, and Saltvik keeps the old pan-workers' cottages.

Vindalen Vindalen

The valley where the vine lives: grain on the hills, wine in the dale, and the long lunches of the south.

Fish and grain

The East

Easthaven Easthaven

The fishers sail before dawn, and the morning markets eat well before noon.

Rullarkuller Woldham

The rolling hills carry grain to the sea; Woldham holds the grain exchange and the harvest festival.

Mund-av-Lin Linmouth

The mouth of the Lin: reed water, birds, and river pilots — and Linbridge at the last dry crossing.

Harbour and forest

The West

Belhaven Belhaven

The deep-water port of the west — French shutters over English brick, and lights on the sea at night.

Torkadeland Moorgate

The parched land: heath, dry-stone walls, and sun — and Moorgate with its patient market.

The Forests Pineforest

Cork-oak and pine from valley to ridge. Pineforest lives in the forest, and the forest lives on.

Cliffs and ferries

The North

Highcliffe Highcliffe

The chalk cliffs of the north coast, the lighthouses on them, and the gulls by the sea.

Lakmorgen Whitmere

The lake country: water and light at morning, and Whitmere on the largest of the meres.

The Northern Straits Sundby

The ferries sail from Sundby to the islands — winter and summer, night and day.

Out at sea

The Isles

Sainte-Agathe Shellharbour

The scattered outer isles: small harbours, chapel bells, and long days. The ferry from Belhaven takes you to Shellharbour — one ticket, boat included (Art. 14).
