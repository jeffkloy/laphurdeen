From many shores, onto three hills

The Founders' Hills

When the four fleets sailed into the bay, the founders built their first houses on the hills above the water. The city's name remembers it: French «la pente», “the slope,” plus the Old Charter suffix -ieur — the place of slopes, where every street climbs.

Today Grundarkulleren — the Founders' Hills — are the heart of the capital: streets that climb, sea-fog in the morning, stairways between the houses, and the whole bay glittering below after dark. Walk slowly. The city was built for it.

Sjartakullen Charter Hill

Where the founders signed the Founding Charter — and where Parliament stands today. The hill of the law, where all power comes from the people.

Sterkullen Star Hill

The highest hill carries the lighthouse: the light that led the Charter fleet home through the night, and that stands on the flag as the white star.

Mistkullen Fog Hill

The hill of the morning fog, and of the university — tuition capped by the Constitution itself (Article 11). From its park you can see the whole bay.

Open public life

The Anchor on The Hill

Before Parliament on Sjartakullen stands the first anchor of the Charter fleet — which is how the capital got its byname. The central houses of government cluster here, and every door of them is open: transparency isn't a promise in Laphurdeen, it's Articles 32 and 33.

Parliament

The Senate and the Commons sit on Sjartakullen. Every vote of every member is recorded in the open (Article 20), and the galleries are free — walk in and listen. The Speaker receives ambassadors here too (Article 27).

The twelve ministries

Around Parliament stand the twelve ministries — each run by Lead Ministers the country elects by name (Article 18). No gates, no walls: just doors.

The High Court

The final keeper of the Constitution (Article 31). Every hearing is public — come in and watch the law at work.

The Integrity Commission

The house that watches all the other houses: registers of assets, income, and gifts — open to anyone, any day (Article 33).

Harbour to hilltop, every ten minutes

The Bergbaan

A city of hills needs a railway that climbs: the Bergbaan runs from the harbour to all three hills, every ten minutes. Ferries link the capital to every island of the Commonwealth.

And if you'd rather walk, the old stairways between the houses are a journey in themselves. Fares are capped by law, and anyone in the Laphurdeen Guarantee rides free (Article 14).

See the principal city too: Darcambria, the amber curve — the harbourside city centre and the U-bahn and T-bahn that never sleep.
