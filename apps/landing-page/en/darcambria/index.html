Darcambria

The province that gave the flag its arc and its colour: a great harbour that curves like a crescent moon, amber dusk-light on the water — and 4,900,000 people whose day begins with fish and bread straight from the market.

Harbour first

A province built on the waterline

Everything in Darcambria starts at the harbour. The great curve of quays is the city centre: the fish market opens before the sun does, ferries cross the bay every twenty minutes, and in the evening half the city sits at the water's edge watching the light turn amber — the same light that's on the national flag.

The locals are not modest about it. Ask any fisher about the harbour and you'll hear it's «den BAAT av alle hamner» — the greatest of all harbours. And when something is cheap and good, the highest praise in the city is “like breakfast in Fiskarhamnen.”

Five neighbourhoods, one curve

Where to lose a day

Fiskarhamnen the Fishers’ Harbour

The city's first neighbourhood. The boats go out before sunrise and come back with breakfast for all of Darcambria; the waterside market runs every day of the year.

Gammelstaden the Old Town

Narrow Charter-era lanes, houses in every colour, and little squares where time moves slowly. The best bread in the Commonwealth, the locals say — and they've done the research.

Darstranden the Amber Beach

Sand, waves, and the amber evening light that named the city. In summer the whole town swims here; in winter it walks the waterline instead.

Vinbergen the Wine Hill

The hill above the harbour: wine from its own terraces, and wine houses with the whole bay below. Arrive before the dusk turns amber — stay until the stars take over.

Nyhamn the New Harbour

The harbour's newest arm: old shipping halls turned into workshops for poets and makers — markets, music, and lights on the water long into the night.

Eight towns, one bay

The eight Charter towns

Darcambria is more than the curve. Around the bay and down the coast lie eight Charter cities whose names came ashore with the English fleet — and, like the ph in Laphurdeen, were never touched by the Reform: Darlingmoors, Addison, Marionberry, Upperlea, Lowerlea, Briarside, Winchester-on-the-Sea, and Riverlin.

Each keeps its own heart — a market, a harbour, a school — but together they are the province: the Darcambrian Communities, the D and the C in DCTS. One ticket, one network, one bay. Ask anyone in Briarside where they live, and the answer is “Darcambria.”

Darlingmoors City & Island

The largest of the eight, on its own island in the bay: Seaport Village and Oyster Point, Forest Park and The Beaches, the Yacht Harbour — and the Government Centre, where the eight cities govern together, with the Peak District and the Gorges rising behind. The Commonwealth's largest city: 2,850,000 people. Visit Darlingmoors →

Winchester-on-the-Sea staden ved zeen

Beaches, old houses, sea air: where Darcambrians go for a day on the water — twenty minutes from the city centre on the express.

Addison to Riverlin six towns, one ticket

Addison, Marionberry of the Square, Upperlea and Lowerlea on their hills, Briarside at the end of the shoreline — and little Riverlin, smallest in headcount and second in density: the most town per street in the Commonwealth.

Article 14, every five minutes

The U-bahn and the T-bahn

Public transport is a constitutional right in Laphurdeen (Article 14) — and in Darcambria it's a point of civic pride. Two networks, one ticket, no car required. (The spelling bahn is Charter-era license for baan, “track” — the same license that keeps the ph in Laphurdeen.)

U-bahn — trains beneath the city

U1 U2 U3

Three lines bind the neighbourhoods together: U1 runs beneath the great curve from Fiskarhamnen to Nyhamn, U2 climbs to Vinbergen, and U3 heads out to Darstranden. Trains every five minutes, every day.

T-bahn — trams in the daylight

T1 T2 T3 T4 T5

Five lines run along the waterfront and through the old streets — the best moving view of the city there is. Cars every eight minutes from morning to midnight.

DCTS — everyone says “the T”

U4 U5 R6 R7

The three tunnel lines under the city are only the heart of it. The Darcambrian Communities Transport Service carries the network out to the eight towns: U4, the Harbour Line, to the Yacht Harbour and the airport; U5, the University Line; R6 and R7 through the villages of Cambrian Inlet — plus four expresses at the busy hours, calling at Central, Market Square, and the Old Courthouse. All of it on the one capped ticket — only the Airport Link carries its own premium: kr. 320.

And the fare?

Capped by law so that everyone can afford it, and free for anyone in the Laphurdeen Guarantee (Article 14). One ticket covers U-bahn, T-bahn, and the ferries together — tax included in the displayed price, as always (Article 35(4)).

See the capital too: Lapentieur, The Anchor on The Hill — the Founders' Hills and the open houses of government.
