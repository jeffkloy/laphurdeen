Darlingmoors

The city is the island, and the island is the city: 2,850,000 people across five zones and a City Centre - the largest city in the Commonwealth, at the heart of the amber curve.

From Seaport Village to Cambrian Junction

Five zones and a city heart

Every neighbourhood keeps its number under the 0221 dialling plan - and its own flavour.

City Centre 0221 2

The city's heart: Central, Market Square, and Old Courthouse - where the mainline trains come in and the city begins.

C - by the harbour 0221 3

Outer Docks and Inner Docks, the Government Centre where the eight cities govern together, and Yacht Harbour with its waterside tables.

Northwest 0221 4

Seaport Village and its songs, Darling Hill and Outlook with their views, and Timberland at the forest's edge.

Northeast 0221 5

Oyster Point, Forest Park, and The Beaches: oysters by the sea, forest down to the shore, and sand for everyone.

Southwest 0221 6

Peak District, Eastern Peak, Inner Gorge and Outer Gorge: the mountain inside the city, and the high steps above it.

Southeast 0221 7

Southeast Slopes, Shoreline Rocks, and Cambrian Junction - where the music lives after dark.

Market Square

The Exchange, the banks, and the Mint

Market Square is where the Commonwealth's money works - and where the three words go onto every krona. The quarter keeps market hours: the Square's morning bell still opens trading, and the bell is older than the country.

The Bors

On the Darlingmoors Exchange, grain, salt, and shipping find their price - as in the first days, under the amber-glass dome.

The banks

The country's bank headquarters stand along Market Square, house by house - English brick over French stone.

The Mint

The Mint strikes three words onto every krona - Frihed, Velvard, Konsens - and the Reserve Bank holds the peg fast (Art. 38).

Old Courthouse and Yacht Harbour

Art for all the shores

Old Courthouse gave the law to the arts: the courthouse is now the gallery, and the water at Yacht Harbour is the theatre.

Galleri av de Mange Strander

The national collection in the old courthouse - with the small museums for the Charter fleet and the Language Commission's first words around it.

The Harbour Opera

The Harbour Opera sits on the mole with the bay for a backdrop - the city's best night for a sjanson.

The Amber Hall

Concerts for the eight cities - and the free festivals at Government Centre, with eight choirs singing the anthem at once on Charter Days.

Cambrian Junction and Seaport Village

Two sides of the music

One side is loud and late; the other is quiet and old. Both are Darlingmoors.

Cambrian Junction

The old sheds along the freight wye are the venues now: loud music, long nights, and the walls painted by whoever played last.

Seaport Village

Folk rooms over the chandleries: old songs in three languages, from the families who came with the ferries.

From Oyster Point to Yacht Harbour

The city eats well

Darlingmoors eats in the street and dines on the water: oysters from Oyster Point, fish from the market halls, wine from Vindalen.

Oyster Point

Oysters by the dozen with Saltvik salt and Vindalen white, straight off the transom - the morning boats sell at the point.

The market halls

Behind Market Square: morning fish, bread and grain, Saltvik salt - and the eight cities' eight rival versions of the same fish soup.

The Beaches boardwalk

Fish in paper cones on the sand, and the sun going down amber over the sea. No cutlery.

Yacht Harbour

The restoran row: high register in both languages - and the tax already in the price, as always (Art. 35(4)).

Northeast and Southeast

The city's beaches

A metropolis with sand inside it: three beaches, three flavours, one sea.

The Beaches

Three kilometres of city sand - the U2 and R6 run straight to it, under the fare cap as always.

Shoreline Rocks

Deep coves and diving stone - the swimmers' sea, winter and summer.

Forest Park

The pines walk down to the sand: no casino, no music - just the sea.
